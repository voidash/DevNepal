About · How to contribute

Nepal’s trusted public-interest developer network

Government institutions open suitable technology projects to contribution; developers, designers, writers and testers build capability, reputation and community by delivering visible public value. DevNepal is the registry and collaboration layer — the code stays on GitHub.

01 · The process
1
A ministry lists a project

A named officer completes a suitability checklist — legal authority, rights, data classification (only Public is eligible), security, maintainer capacity — and submits it.

2
PMO approves

Super Admin reviews completeness and suitability. Only approved listings carry the Official badge. Decisions are attributable and logged.

3
You contribute

Apply to a workstream or pick a labelled starter task. Code goes through GitHub pull requests; design, documentation, QA, translation and research are submitted as evidence.

4
Work is verified and recognised

A maintainer accepts your work. The record appears on your profile with who accepted it and how it arrived; scoring follows the public policy.

02 · What DevNepal is — and is not
It is
  • A public catalog of approved government projects and member projects
  • Identity, structured project metadata, applications and participation records
  • Verified contribution records, badges and a quality-based leaderboard
  • Technical blogs, moderation, audit trails and reporting
It is not
  • A code host — no branches, issues, pull requests or CI here
  • Procurement, tendering, employment, payroll, grants or bounties
  • A social network — no private messaging, follower feeds or chat
  • A place where volunteers get production access or code is auto-merged

04 · Frequently asked
Do I need GitHub?

Only for code. Design, documentation, QA, translation and research are submitted as evidence and verified the same way.

Who verifies my work?

A named maintainer of the ministry project — never the platform, never you. Each record shows who accepted it and how it arrived (GitHub event or ministry attestation).

What is public about me?

Your name, username and skills by default; verified contributions and badges. Email, phone and sign-in provider are never public. You choose the rest, field by field.

Can ministries see my email?

No. When you apply, they see your public profile and your answers to their screening questions — nothing else.

How are scores calculated?

By the public scoring policy: merged pull requests, accepted evidence, closed issues, approved reviews. Commits, bots and self-reported activity are never scored.

How do I report a problem?

Every profile, project, post and link has a Report action with structured reasons. Security vulnerabilities go to the security contact, not the community queue.

How does my ministry join?

The Secretary nominates a named officer by letter to PMO; PMO provisions the organization and invites the officer. No shared logins.

Can I use Nepali everywhere?

Yes. The interface, policies and help are bilingual; ministry titles and summaries are in both languages; you write in the language you choose.
